NSDI 2025: Networked Systems Design & Implementation
Author: Ajay Anubolu Date: 04/30/2025 Location: Chapel Hill, NC
Note: The sessions detailed below represent a selected sample of the talks attended during the conference. These were chosen to highlight key industry shifts relevant to the foundation's technical roadmap.

Executive Summary & Trends

NSDI 2025 marked a distinct shift in the industry towards "System-ML Convergence." The overarching theme of the conference was that networking, hardware, and AI can no longer be optimized in silos. The most impactful sessions focused on how distributed systems must evolve to support the massive computational requirements of Large Language Model (LLM) training and inference. Key tracks included Infrastructure for Machine Learning, Machine Learning for Networks, and Operational Experiences.

Sample Technical Highlights

1. Infrastructure for Machine Learning

The rapid growth of AI models has created bottlenecks in data center communication. Several talks addressed this by automating how systems communicate during training.

  • AutoCCL: A standout presentation on automating distributed training communication. The key insight was that static configurations fail because workloads vary wildly; the system achieved ~1.23× bandwidth improvement by dynamically adapting to the workload.
  • SuperServe: This session focused on inference (serving the model to users). It introduced a framework for "instantaneous model switching," allowing a system to react to bursty user demand without the typical lag, balancing accuracy and resource efficiency.

2. Machine Learning for Networks

Beyond using networks to support AI, researchers are now using AI to optimize the networks themselves.

  • Mutant: A Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework for congestion control. It demonstrated how a system can "learn" from existing protocols without needing massive datasets, using Bayesian prediction to manage network traffic more intelligently than static algorithms.

3. Operational Experiences & Hardware

Real-world deployment stories from major tech firms highlighted the physical challenges of scaling AI.

  • Reliability at Scale (Aegis): An analysis of fault detection in large-scale clusters revealed that simple components—like fiber optic links—are often less reliable than the complex active hardware, necessitating better automated fault detection.
  • RDMA Scalability: Sessions on Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA) discussed the "tail latency" problem, where scaling a network up can paradoxically decrease performance if not managed with new transport protocols like the ones proposed in the ScalaCN and Juneberry talks.
GTC 2025: NVIDIA GPU Technology Conference
Author: Ajay Anubolu Date: 03/19/2025 Focus: AI, Robotics, Edge Compute
Note: The following is a summary of key themes and specific session examples selected from the broader conference agenda to demonstrate alignment with our open-source AI initiatives.

Executive Summary & Trends

GTC 2025 focused heavily on the concept of the "End-to-End AI Lifecycle." The conference emphasized that AI development is becoming a closed feedback loop: training foundation models in data centers, verifying them in high-fidelity physics simulations, and deploying them to the real world. A major new focus was "Agentic AI"—systems that can reason, plan, and use tools autonomously, rather than just answering questions.

Sample Technical Highlights

1. The Edge-to-Cloud Lifecycle

Sessions on the Isaac Platform demonstrated how the "sim-to-real" gap is being closed. By using "Inside-out" AI (agents trained in simulation), developers can validate robot behavior in a virtual world (Omniverse) before deploying to physical hardware. This is critical for lowering the cost of robotics research.

2. Autonomous Systems (AV 2.0)

The automotive track highlighted a move away from heuristic (rule-based) driving to "End-to-End" learning.

  • Cosmos World: A foundation model designed specifically to "reason" about traffic and multi-sensor fusion.
  • Agentic Autonomy: New architectures allow vehicles to function as agents that possess a goal-oriented understanding of their environment, capable of operating in both first-person and third-person perspectives.

3. Optimization & Acceleration

For the foundation's work on efficient computing, the sessions on pipeline optimization were particularly relevant.

  • Visual Search Acceleration: A deep dive into removing CPU bottlenecks from computer vision. Tools like nvImageCodec and cv-cuda were shown to move the entire preprocessing pipeline (decoding, resizing, normalizing) onto the GPU. This allows for significantly higher throughput for semantic search tasks.
OFC 2025: Optical Fiber Communication Conference
Author: Ajay Anubolu Date: 04/03/2025 Focus: Optical Networking
Note: This report highlights selected demonstrations and technical sessions that illustrate the physical infrastructure requirements for next-generation computing facilities.

Executive Summary & Trends

OFC 2025 provided a look at the "plumbing" that makes modern AI possible. The conference focused on the transition to 800G and 1.6T Ethernet technologies. The primary takeaway is that as electrical signaling hits physical limits, optical networking is moving closer to the chip (Co-Packaged Optics) and thermal management is becoming a networking problem, not just a cooling problem.

Sample Technical Highlights

1. Emerging Trends in Connectivity

The exhibition floor showcased the hardware necessary to support the massive bandwidth of H100/Blackwell-class GPU clusters.

  • High-Speed Transceivers: Demonstrations of PAM4-based optical modules supporting 800G and 1.6T throughput. These are essential for the "East-West" traffic patterns seen in distributed training.
  • Liquid-Cooled Switching: Vendors like Supermicro demonstrated switch chassis where the optical switch itself is liquid-cooled, allowing for higher density 51.2 Tbps ASICs without overheating.

2. Optical Interconnects & Architecture

Sessions explored how the architecture of the data center is changing.

  • Co-Packaged Optics (CPO): A recurring theme was the integration of the optical engine directly onto the switch ASIC to reduce power consumption and latency.
  • Silicon Photonics: New switching technologies using photonic integrated circuits (PICs) promise near-zero latency reconfiguration for optical circuit switching.

3. Physical Media Deep Dive

Understanding the physical layer is crucial for our lab setups. I examined various cable architectures, including the trade-offs between Single-Mode Fiber (for longer reach), Multi-Mode Fiber (for intra-rack), and Active Optical Cables (AOCs) for plug-and-play high-bandwidth connections.
